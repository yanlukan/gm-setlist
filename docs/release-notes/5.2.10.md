# PlayBook 5.2.10: no part starts with the chords of the part before, no holes in a Stage row

After the rehearsal of 2026-11-24 the player said Kissing a Fool was confusing to read and play: a whole line held just two chords, and in other songs parts looked as if they began with chords moved from the part before. Both were true, and they had two causes.

## 1. Bars cut off from their phrase

A bar left over at the end of a phrase had been written as the first bar of the next part, or as a part of its own. No chord and no bar changed; they are grouped differently.

**Kissing a Fool**

- The Verse is three clean rounds of 8 bars (24 bars).
- Its 25th bar and the bar that leads into the Bridge are a two-bar turnaround. They are now a **Link** of their own, so the Bridge starts on its own first bar and is 8 bars.
- The same two bars before the Middle 8 are **Link 2**: the same as the Link without its last chord. The Middle 8 is 8 bars and the two-bar break.
- The Last verse is 4 bars; its fifth bar is the Outro's first bar, where the verse's loop would start again. The Outro is 4 bars and 3.
- Song order: Intro, Verse, Link, Bridge, Verse, Link 2, Middle 8, Last verse, Outro.

**I Can't Make You Love Me**

- The chorus ends on the intro's four bars. Their last bar had been cut off: a one-bar "Chorus end" the first time, and the first bar of the Outro the second time.
- Now there is a **Chorus** (ends on a whole bar of Am7, back to the verse) and a **Last chorus** (ends on Em7 Am7, into the Outro), each with its last line whole. The Outro starts on its own first bar.

**Club Tropicana**

- The two-bar "Walk-up" was the end of the last verse's fourth time round. It is the Last verse's last line again, which is now a whole four bars.

A new test keeps it so: no part of a built-in song may open with a single bar on a line of its own.

## 2. Holes in the middle of a Stage row

On an iPad on its side, Stage draws eight bars a row, two written lines side by side. A line of one or two bars sat in its own half of the row with a hole after it, and the next line started past the hole.

Now a line of one or two bars is followed straight away by the next line when both fit in the row, with the strong phrase line between them. Blank cells only come at the end of a row. This changes Everything She Wants (Intro) and Somebody to Love (Verse, Solo, Outro) on Stage.

The iPad upright, Practice and a phone keep a row for each written line, as before.
